Choosing an ITSM platform: the criteria grid

Six criteria genuinely separate ITSM platforms. The rest waste time in a tender process.

Claire Vasseur Service management consultant

In short:

  1. Six criteria genuinely separate ITSM platforms, and functional richness is not among them.
  2. On core processes, incident, request and change, every serious platform performs alike.
  3. The billing unit, technician or end user, weighs more on five-year cost than any single feature.
  4. The service catalogue is written before the consultation, not after: it is what makes demos comparable.

What to settle before looking at any tool

Most tenders start with a feature grid sent to vendors. That is the reverse of the order that produces a good decision. Until scope is written down, every vendor answers on its own ground and the answers are not comparable.

The real scope

Three questions frame it: how many departments will eventually be served, is asset management in scope, and who will operate the platform three years from now. The answers determine half the criteria below. Scoping is covered in our article on the fundamentals of Enterprise Service Management.

The six criteria that decide

CriterionWhat to checkWhy it decides
Asset managementBuilt in or a separate moduleA separate component forces permanent data reconciliation
Multiple cataloguesAutonomous publishing per departmentPrecondition for any extension beyond IT
Billing unitPer technician or per end userMajor gap in five-year cost
Rights modelPartitioning of ticket contentWithout it, HR and legal use cases are ruled out
Data migrationImport format, history, attachmentsLost history resets every indicator to zero
HostingSaaS, managed or on premisesDetermines who carries upgrades and backup

The fourth is the one most often discovered too late. A platform unable to hide ticket content from unrelated teams will never serve human resources, whatever its other qualities. The topic is developed in our comparison of ESM and ITSM.

The criteria that decide nothing

  • Feature count. Every serious platform handles an incident, a request and a change
  • Advertised ITIL compliance. The framework does not certify software, only people and management systems
  • The demo. It is built around cases that work well, which is fair enough, but it says nothing about yours

How to run the comparison

  1. Write the catalogue of the ten most frequent requests, with their real approval paths
  2. Have each vendor replay those ten cases in their own tool, unrehearsed
  3. Cost it over five years, licences, hosting, integration and operations included

The third step holds surprises in both directions. A platform billed per technician can stay cheaper than an apparently free solution that is poorly tooled, and the reverse is just as true. The same full-cost reasoning applies to IT inventory, whose collection is often billed separately.

Frequently asked questions

How do you choose an ITSM platform?

The decision rests on six criteria: native asset management, support for several partitioned service catalogues, the licence billing unit, the granularity of the rights model, migration of existing data, and hosting mode. On core processes, incident, request and change, functional gaps between serious platforms are too small to decide anything. The length of a feature list is therefore not a selection criterion.

Should you choose the tool before or after writing your service catalogue?

After. A written catalogue lets you test platforms against real cases rather than rehearsed demos, and it immediately exposes the limits of the rights model.

How many platforms should be shortlisted?

Three is enough when they have been pre-screened on the eliminating criteria. Beyond that, evaluation time grows without improving the decision.
