ITIL certifications: levels and pathways

ITIL certifications explained: the ITIL 4 scheme, version 5, and why the widely repeated five-level model belongs to a withdrawn version.

  1. An ITIL certification validates knowledge of a service management framework, not mastery of a tool.
  2. The ITIL 4 scheme rests on a single foundation, then on designations obtained by accumulating modules and a Master level based on experience.
  3. A version 5 of the framework is rolling out in phases, with a simplified qualification structure and a bridge module for ITIL 4 certificate holders.
  4. The widely repeated five-level model describes a withdrawn version of the framework.

Looking for information on ITIL certifications quickly runs into a problem: the best-ranked pages do not all describe the same scheme. Some list five successive levels, others speak of designations, others still announce a new version. This confusion is not harmless, as it leads candidates to aim for a title that is no longer awarded. Setting the scheme straight means separating what belongs to the current framework, to its ongoing evolution, and to withdrawn versions whose documentation keeps circulating.

What an ITIL certification actually validates

ITIL is a framework of good practice for IT service management. It describes a shared vocabulary, guiding principles and a set of management practices, without imposing any tool or organisational model. A certification therefore attests to knowledge of that framework, not to competence on a given platform.

The distinction has concrete consequences. A certified professional can name the objects they handle correctly, place a request in the right process and understand what a service commitment covers. They cannot, on that basis alone, configure a specific tool. This is why certification generally comes alongside operational experience rather than replacing it. The seven guiding principles of ITIL 4 form the core of what the entry exam checks.

The exam is administered by the body that owns the framework, independently of the candidate’s employer. That is what gives the certification its portability from one organisation to another.

The ITIL 4 certification scheme

The scheme in force since 2019 abandons the idea of a single ladder. It rests on a mandatory foundation, then on designations obtained by accumulating several modules.

ITIL 4 Foundation, the only entry point

Foundation is the only module available without prerequisites. It covers the vocabulary, the four dimensions of service management, the service value system and the most common practices, including incident and problem management. It is the module the vast majority of candidates aim for, and the only one required by most job adverts that mention ITIL.

Managing Professional, Strategic Leader and Practice Manager

Beyond the foundation, three designations cover three different orientations. Managing Professional addresses professionals who run services day to day, from design through to support. Strategic Leader covers the alignment between the information system and the organisation’s strategy. Practice Manager validates in-depth mastery of a set of management practices.

Each is obtained by passing several modules rather than a single exam. The strategy module within the Strategic Leader designation additionally requires at least three years of managerial experience.

The Master level

The Master level is not sat as an exam. It is awarded to professionals who have obtained the preceding designations and can demonstrate real application of the framework. There is therefore no Master exam to prepare for, which explains the very small number of holders.

Version 5 of the framework and its modules

A new version of the framework is rolling out in phases, with modules and pathways introduced progressively. It claims a simpler qualification structure and takes account of practices linked to artificial intelligence.

The modules of the new structure

Foundation remains the entry point. The higher modules carry functional names, covering product, service, experience, transformation and strategy. They feed the same three designations as before: Practice Manager, Managing Professional and Strategic Leader. The transformation module has a useful particularity: it is common to every designation and is sat only once, its result counting towards each of them. An extension module devoted to the governance of artificial intelligence completes the set.

The bridge module for ITIL 4 certificate holders

Holders of an ITIL 4 certification do not have to sit the foundation again. A bridge module, focused on the new material alone, allows a move to the new Foundation. ITIL 4 certifications also remain recognised as prerequisites for the higher modules. No certification already obtained becomes void because of this change of version.

Why the five-level scheme is still circulating

Many pages, including some of the best ranked, present ITIL certifications as a five-rung ladder: Foundation, Practitioner, Intermediate, Expert, Master, with a credit system to accumulate on the way to Expert.

That model did exist, but it belongs to ITIL v3, a withdrawn version. The Practitioner, Intermediate and Expert titles are no longer awarded under the current scheme. The content describing them has simply not been updated, and its age often gives it good ranking, which keeps the confusion alive.

The practical consequence is straightforward: a candidate who sets out to become an “ITIL Expert” is aiming at a title that is no longer awarded. Checking the publication date of the page being read avoids that kind of error before committing to a pathway. The same caution applies to the invented labels occasionally encountered, with some articles announcing versions of the framework that were never published.

Which level to aim for by role

For a support technician, a service desk agent or an administrator, Foundation is enough in almost every case. It provides the shared vocabulary that teams often lack, and explains why an organisation distinguishes a service desk from a help desk.

For a service manager, a project manager or a consultant, a full designation makes sense, provided the matching practice is there. Advanced modules probe management situations rather than definitions, and purely theoretical preparation works poorly.

For an executive team, the strategic orientation is the most coherent, particularly in organisations extending service management practices beyond IT. That extension connects directly to the differences between ESM and ITSM.

Training and sitting the exam

The role of accredited organisations

Training is delivered by accredited organisations, which buy the right to prepare candidates for the exams and set their own prices. Accreditation guarantees that the content conforms to the official body of knowledge; it says nothing about teaching quality or price. The total cost therefore always breaks down into two distinct lines, training on one side and the exam on the other.

Sitting an exam without attending training remains possible for candidates preparing on their own, which is the most economical option at Foundation level.

What preparation covers

Effective preparation combines three elements: reading the official body of knowledge, sustained practice on mock exams, and connecting the material to real situations. Exams test the framework as written, not the candidate’s professional habits, which is the most common trap for experienced profiles. Answering from practice rather than from the framework costs marks.

Format and pass mark vary by module and by version. They are specified at registration, the only reliable source on that point.

Frequently asked questions

What is an ITIL certification?

An ITIL certification confirms that a person has mastered the vocabulary, principles and practices of the ITIL service management framework. It is obtained by sitting an exam administered by the body that owns the framework, independently of the employer and of the tool used day to day. It validates knowledge of the methodological framework, not competence on a particular piece of software nor field experience, except at the highest level of the scheme.

How many levels does the ITIL certification scheme have?

The current scheme is not read as a series of successive levels but as a foundation plus designations. A single entry exam, Foundation, opens access to specialised designations obtained by accumulating modules, then to a Master level based on experience. The linear five-rung model still found in many articles describes a withdrawn version of the framework.

How much does an ITIL certification cost?

The cost breaks down into two distinct parts: the exam fee, set by the certification body, and the training fee, set freely by the accredited organisation delivering it. Both vary by country, by module and by the format chosen. Sitting the exam alone, without training, remains possible for candidates who prepare on their own.

How should someone prepare for an ITIL certification?

Preparation combines reading the official body of knowledge, practising on mock exams and, in most cases, training with an accredited organisation. Advanced modules also assume real practice, because their questions address management situations rather than definitions.

Does an ITIL 4 certification remain valid under version 5?

Yes. ITIL 4 certifications retain their value and are recognised as prerequisites for the higher modules of the new version. A bridge module also allows ITIL 4 certificate holders to update on the new material alone, without sitting the whole foundation again.
